Board Basics: Choosing and Cleaning Wood vs. Plastic Cutting Boards

By Isabella Rossini • 7 Min Read

Every home cook needs a cutting board. It protects your kitchen counters from knife damage, provides a clean workspace, and prevents knife blades from dulling quickly. However, choosing between wood and plastic cutting boards—and cleaning them correctly—raises many questions. Which material is more hygienic? How do you prevent cross-contamination? Can you wash a wood board in the dishwasher? Understanding material properties, knife-edge wear, and sanitation methods is the key to managing cutting board hygiene. This guide compares wood and plastic boards to help you choose the best option for your kitchen.

1. Wood Cutting Boards: Natural Protection

Wood is the traditional choice for cutting boards. Hardwoods (like maple, walnut, or cherry) feature dense fibers that offer several benefits:

  • Knife-Edge Friendly: Wood fibers separate slightly under a knife blade, absorbing the impact and preventing the steel edge from dulling.
  • Natural Hygiene: Wood features natural capillary action that draws moisture and bacteria deep into the wood structure, where the bacteria die due to lack of oxygen and moisture.
  • Esthetics: A high-quality wood cutting board is beautiful and can be used as a serving platter.
"Cutting board hygiene is about moisture management. Wood boards protect knife edges and self-sanitize, but require hand washing and regular oiling."

2. Plastic Cutting Boards: Practical Utility

Plastic boards (made from high-density polyethylene) are affordable, lightweight, and easy to clean:

  • Dishwasher Safe: Unlike wood, plastic boards can handle the high heat and water exposure of a dishwasher, making them easy to sanitize.
  • Color Coding: Home cooks use different colored plastic boards to prevent cross-contamination (for example, green for vegetables, red for raw meat).
  • The Wear Risk: Over time, knife blades create deep grooves in plastic boards. These micro-scratches trap food particles and bacteria, making the board difficult to clean. When a plastic board develops deep grooves, it should be replaced.

3. Cleaning and Sanitation Guidelines

Maintain your boards correctly to prevent cross-contamination:

Washing Wood Boards (Never use the dishwasher)

Wash your wood board by hand using warm water and a mild dish soap. Dry the board immediately with a towel and let it air dry standing upright to prevent warping. Never soak a wood board in a sink of water or wash it in the dishwasher, as the high heat and moisture will cause the wood to warp and crack.

Sanitizing Both Boards

To sanitize your boards after preparing raw meat, wash with warm soapy water, then wipe with a mild bleach solution (1 tablespoon of unscented liquid bleach per gallon of water). Let the solution sit for a few minutes before rinsing thoroughly with clean water.

4. Oiling Wood Cutting Boards

To keep a wood cutting board from drying and cracking, apply a food-grade **mineral oil** (never vegetable or olive oil, which can spoil and turn rancid) once a month. Rub the oil into the wood with a cloth, let it sit overnight, and wipe away any excess in the morning.

At Happy Kitchen Guide, we recommend using a large hardwood board for vegetables and bread, and color-coded plastic boards for raw meats. By understanding material wear, washing boards safely, and oiling wood regularly, you can maintain hygiene in your kitchen. Protect your knives, wash your boards correctly, and enjoy prep work.
